01 · Overview

One framework, three interaction domains, eight modalities.

Modern pharmacology treats drug-related interactions — Drug-Drug (DDI), Drug-Target (DTI) and Protein-Protein (PPI) — as a single connected ecosystem. OpenDrug is the first benchmark that operationalises this view: a multimodal heterogeneous knowledge graph, standardised splits, rigorous stress tests, and mechanistic interpretation.

The three-stage pipeline

  1. A

    Heterogeneous graph construction

    Drug and protein nodes are embedded with a 5+3 modality alignment (SMILES, 2D graph, 3D conformer, text and pathway for drugs; sequence, text and 3D structure for proteins) and linked by DDI, DTI and PPI edges.

  2. B

    Multi-task benchmarking

    30 representative baselines are profiled across 7 task types: binary / multi-class / multi-label classification and affinity regression. Efficiency profiling (time, memory) ships in the same harness.

  3. C

    Biomedical insight extraction

    Cold-start, label / topology / feature noise, and modality-ablation stress tests quantify robustness. Attention-based subgraph mining then traces predictions back to mechanistic rationales.

04 · Robustness

Stringent stress tests expose the memorisation bottleneck.

Real drug discovery is full of unseen drugs, sparse modalities and noisy experimental data. OpenDrug profiles every baseline under cold-start (drug / protein / blind), label noise, topology noise, feature noise and modality ablation.

−25%

Performance drop observed across SOTA models under cold-start scenarios, exposing a pervasive memorisation bottleneck.

+5 to 20%

Consistent performance gains from comprehensive multimodal alignment, mathematically validating the 5+3 design.

0 → 0.8

Label and topology noise sweeps reveal a clear robustness hierarchy: ColdstartCPI stays stable while MKG-FENN collapses under heavy edge drop.

05 · Mechanism

From graph topology to molecular rationale.

Attention-based subgraph mining reverses high-confidence predictions back to clinically recognisable mechanisms. Three case studies illustrate the translation from graph language to pharmacological language.

DDI

CYP3A4 target competition

Simvastatin and Ketoconazole both dock into the CYP3A4 catalytic site. The model recovers this polypharmacy clash and flags rhabdomyolysis risk — a textbook adverse drug reaction.

  • Drugs: Simvastatin · Ketoconazole
  • Enzyme: CYP3A4
  • Outcome: Rhabdomyolysis & acute renal failure
DTI

PDE5 / PDE6 cross-reactivity

Sildenafil was designed for PDE5 but also binds PDE6 due to near-identical binding pockets. The subgraph ranks the off-target edge as highly attended — matching the cyanopsia side effect seen in the clinic.

  • Drug: Sildenafil
  • Targets: PDE5 (intended) · PDE6 (off-target)
  • Outcome: Transient cyanopsia
PPI

Hsp90–Cdc37–CDK4 chaperone network

CDK4 maturation is inefficient without the co-chaperone Cdc37, which scans and docks the kinase onto Hsp90. The mined subgraph reproduces this classical assembly pathway with high-attention weights on all three nodes.

  • Chaperone: Hsp90 · Cdc37
  • Client kinase: CDK4
  • Outcome: Oncogenic kinase maturation
06 · Cognitive boundaries

Where current models collectively fail.

Through in-depth error analysis we identified three absolute failure domains that span 2D-based and sequence-based architectures. These are the open challenges for the next generation of drug-interaction models.

DDI · Chirality

Thalidomide enantiomers

Both enantiomers share identical 2D graphs. Only the S-enantiomer fits the CRBN hydrophobic tripod pocket, yet 2D GNNs treat them as indistinguishable.

DTI · Mutations

BCR-ABL · T315I resistance

A single gatekeeper mutation (Thr→Ile) introduces steric clash that evicts Imatinib from the ATP pocket. Sequence models cannot model this 3D deformation.

PPI · IDP interfaces

p53–MDM2 plug-and-socket

Three hotspot residues (Phe19, Trp23, Leu26) plug into MDM2's hydrophobic groove. Flat macromolecular interfaces defeat models relying on global homology.

Call to action

To resolve chirality, steric clashing and allosteric regulation, future drug-interaction models must move from static 2D heterogeneous graphs to 3D geometric deep learning, integrating dynamic conformational knowledge from multimodal structural biology.
